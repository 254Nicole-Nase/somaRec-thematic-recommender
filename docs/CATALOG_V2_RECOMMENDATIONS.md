# Feasibility of content-aware recommendations

The Phase 3 prototype did not build a retrieval model or ingest text. Phase 4 adds a [metadata-only offline evaluation](CATALOG_V2_EDITORIAL.md). Its lexical diagnostics have no human relevance judgments. Excerpt and full-text experiments remain future work.

| Approach | Representation | Likely advantage and primary risk |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A. Verified metadata | BM25 over verified titles, authors, aliases and editorial summaries. Add a multilingual dense vector for each synopsis and structured fields for theme, form and language. | Broadest initial coverage and easy explanations. Thin or biased descriptions will limit nuanced matches. Start here and retain a reviewed baseline. |
| B. A plus authorized excerpts | Add passage vectors and evidence-linked literary features for an excerpt of known length and context. Keep metadata results available across all books. | Can surface stylistic or setting signals. Opening chapters and publisher-selected excerpts can underrepresent the rest of the book. |
| C. A plus authorized full text | Embed sections of an exact edition, aggregate complementary passages and extract candidate features from sampled chapters. | May help nuanced thematic or narrative discovery. Full text adds processing cost, licensing and deletion obligations, and the potential for incidental passages or vocabulary to overpower the reader’s intent. |

## Design of an offline comparison

- **Chunk by structure first.** Keep poems intact where practical. For prose, split by chapter and paragraph, then try 300–500 model tokens with about 50 tokens of overlap as an initial setting. Preserve edition, expression, language and chapter/offset provenance. Use the tokenizer for the chosen model. Keep passage content and model logs controlled. Strip OCR noise, running headers, footers and boilerplate. Sample across chapters to avoid indexing only the opening.
- **Compare book representations.** Try normalized means of chapter vectors, and a fixed small number of representative passages per work. At query time cap contributions per work, aggregate a fixed top-k or robust average, and deduplicate editions in ranking. Comparing a query to every chunk and taking the maximum systematically gives longer books more chances. Calibrate scores and minimum evidence on a validation split.
- **Keep structured literary evidence.** Separate setting, historical period, mood, narrative voice and style from topics, plot and character relationships. Annotate candidate features with chapter or editorial source and review state. For explanations use only verified, spoiler-safe features. A statement such as “both explore how land ownership shapes a community” needs support for both works. Exclude endings and reveals from public explanations. Do not reproduce copyrighted passages as evidence in an explanation.
- **Benchmark multilingual retrieval locally.** Compare BM25 with accent-aware normalization, multilingual E5 and BGE-M3 on Kiswahili, English and mixed-language queries. Preserve original spellings and test other local languages once there are enough judgments. Benchmark papers motivate model selection but do not establish quality on this catalog. Fuse lexical and dense scores with reciprocal rank fusion or calibrated ranking. Use explicit filters for requested languages and forms. Treat missing text as missing evidence, and return weak-match or browse fallbacks. [Multilingual E5](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.05672) · [BGE-M3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.03216) · [MTEB](https://aclanthology.org/2023.eacl-main.148/)
- **Separate access and processing rights.** Before any B or C trial, obtain a rights decision for the exact asset and edition that covers access, processing territory, storage, embeddings and feature derivation, authorized processors, display and sharing, retention, expiry and removal. Check translations and images separately. Enforce it before downloading or building features. Keep private text in controlled storage, attribute licensed materials, track derived artifact lineage and test removal from the index and caches. An online preview or bought ebook is insufficient evidence. [Source and rights review](CATALOG_V2_SOURCES.md)

## Human evaluation and decision

Begin with at least 30 authored queries across known titles, book-to-book similarity, themes, form, setting and mood. Include English, Kiswahili and mixed-language queries plus sparse and ambiguous requests. Use multiple raters familiar with the literature. Pool the top results from A, B and C and show them in randomized order without system labels. Rate relevance on a short ordinal scale and judge factual explanation support, specificity and spoiler exposure independently. Record disagreements and adjudication. Calibrate weights using a separate validation subset.

Compare the same eligible work set and the same metadata across A, B and C to isolate the effect of content. Also compare the entire catalog with the real missing-text pattern because licensing affects coverage. Report nDCG@5 and recall over judged results, unique authors/forms and language coverage, duplicate rate, explanation support and spoiler rate, alongside indexed tokens, processing time, latency, storage, estimated cost and was-text-available rate. Report results by language and form with uncertainty. Strong results on a collection of authorized children’s stories cannot justify claims about modern adult fiction. Proceed beyond A only if the measured gains and coverage warrant the rights, editorial and infrastructure costs.
