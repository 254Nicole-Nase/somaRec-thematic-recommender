# Catalog V2 model, verification pipeline and roadmap

The catalog can cover fiction, poetry, drama, children’s books, memoir and other nonfiction. Feature Kenyan literature and keep clearly labeled collections for nonfiction. Accept at least one evidenced inclusion basis, such as a Kenyan author, a Kenyan publication, or a documented Kenyan literary connection. Preserve the basis so readers and editors can distinguish those collections. Exclude a study guide, report, or item merely mentioning Kenya from featured literary collections unless an editor makes the case for it.

## Proposed data model

This model describes relationships and invariants. It does not require production schema changes during prototyping.

| Entity | Fields and integrity rules |
| --- | --- |
| Person and contribution | Stable person ID, display name, aliases, authority IDs, source references. Contributions link to work or edition with role and sequence. Authors, translators, editors and illustrators have separate roles. A name string and a nationality are never person keys. |
| Work | Stable work ID, canonical title, language-tagged alternate titles, original language claim, form and genres, Kenya inclusion basis. Publication years, editions and translations cannot redefine an existing work automatically. Anthologies may link component works. |
| Expression and edition | Expression captures a language version, translator and explicit translation or adaptation relation. Edition captures publisher/imprint, date, territory, format, edition statement, ISBN and its work/expression links. Hardcover and ebook are distinct. ISBN identifies an edition candidate. Do not merge translations or infer an original language from the language of the first returned edition. |
| Source record and identifier assertion | Provider, source type, stable ID, URL, retrieved date, revision or snapshot digest, retention rule and source lineage. Full source payloads are stored only where reuse permits. Identifier claims are namespaced and attached to the entity level actually described. Reused ISBNs and coauthored titles can produce multiple candidates. |
| Field assertion and decision | Typed value, language, entity, evidence IDs, scope, state and review decision. States include unknown, observed, inferred, corroborated, conflicting, and human-reviewed. Keep disagreeing evidence. Treat a source copied from another as one lineage when assessing independence. Prefer publisher for its own dated edition, author or library authorities for identity, and explicitly licensed provenance for text. |
| Synopsis and literary features | Original editorial summaries or approved publisher text with reuse basis and spoiler tier. Keep form and genre separate from themes, subject, setting, historical period, mood, style, and character/narrative features. Attach evidence and a versioned taxonomy. Topic terms inferred from summaries or text remain inference until reviewed. |
| Media and access | Edition-linked cover identifier, creator/credit, display permission and expiry. Separate legal reading, preview, purchase and borrowing links with region and checked date. A catalog link is a lookup, not an availability promise. |
| Licensed text asset and features | Exact work/expression/edition, asset hash and version, excerpt bounds, provenance, licence, territory, allowed storage/processing/hosting/embedding and sharing actions, attribution, retention and removal history. Derived chunks, features and vectors carry asset IDs and model/version so they can be purged or rebuilt. |
| Legacy book mapping | Old UUID, optional work/edition target, candidate reasons, decision, reviewer, and mapping version. Allow unresolved and ambiguous values. Keep linkage available for old URLs and reading actions even when a record is no longer featured. |

For production joins, inventory reading-list rows including default shelves, reviews and per-user uniqueness, CBC alignments, embeddings, and public links. Prefer preserving the served UUID for a reviewed same-book mapping. Resolve two legacy UUIDs for one V2 work case by case, since different editions might be involved and the same user could have two reviews. Any eventual remapping should be reversible, tested transactionally on staging, and retain aliases and reader data. The prototype exports title/author candidates and leaves all mappings pending.

## Reproducible verification pipeline

1. **Select bounded candidates.** Use a literature-aware seed manifest from local presses, national bibliography and archival/author leads. Use Wikidata QIDs and names as discovery inputs, and identify coverage gaps in Kiswahili, Gĩkũyũ, Dholuo, Sheng and other languages explicitly. Do not equate missing citizenship or a diaspora address with non-Kenyan authorship. Record the inclusion decision.
2. **Capture evidence.** Use small identified API requests for exploration, then dated approved snapshots for expansion. Store provider ID, version/date, request reference and a fingerprint of retained allowed facts. Keep raw responses in temporary scratch only when collection is permitted. Retain failures and non-matches. Stop on rate limiting, and honor documented quotas before resuming.
3. **Validate identity.** Match known title and author variants plus typed identifiers. Exclude wrong-title or wrong-author hits. A work or edition cross-link resolves a provider ambiguity only when the link and parent are consistent. Use linked editions for their own publication fields. Keep unrecognized languages, translators and original languages as unknown. When multiple candidates survive, keep competing IDs and quarantine the enrichment or whole work as appropriate.
4. **Classify and curate.** Have genre and language reviewers work against publication evidence. Give drafts their own states. Record publisher/edition evidence for ISBNs and rights provenance for cover, synopsis, links and assets. Reject a description from another title even when a search result was highly ranked. If enrichment is unlicensed or unsupported, omit it. Use original summaries written from verified sources, with a second review before release.
5. **Export and check.** Build stable local IDs and reproducible JSON/CSV from pinned evidence and manifest versions. Report coverage, competing provider hits, identifier collisions, and old UUID candidates. Separate breadth from review fidelity. Expand with incremental differences, reviewer decisions and versioned quality reports. A later production workflow would use a reviewed preview and transactional apply, with an explicit separate decision for merges.

## Strategy

| Option | Strength | Cost and risk |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A. Repair 490 in place | Reuses discovery and current identifiers immediately. | High triage cost for imported metadata with unclear provenance. Hard to reproduce editorial repairs from inconsistent older exports. Retain this for verified high-impact corrections or reader-facing harm. |
| B. Replace all records with a new catalog | Clearer provenance and model from day one. | Drops useful legacy coverage and risks broken readers’ links, shelves and reviews. Matching edition identities and licensing all content remains work even with a clean start. |
| C. Build V2 separately and reconcile gradually | Enables stronger validation and a curated launch without losing legacy identifiers or forcing today’s uncertain merges. | Two catalogs raise review, mapping and versioning costs. Use release gates and explicitly retire old records only after review. |

**Choose C.** Keep the evidence-first manifest and repeatable comparison from this prototype. Favor sources that can grant ongoing access and rights, even if their coverage is narrower. Use the legacy catalog to spot familiar works and mapping hazards. Recommendations will depend on independent relevance judgments and quality of vetted descriptions, not on the count of imported books.

## Milestones and risks

1. **Prototype review now.** Check sources, all inclusion classes and the high-impact mismatches. Verify selected edition facts against publishers or library records. Decide which optional contributor and translation fields to support before accepting feeds.
2. **First 150 verified works as a target.** Recruit Kenyan literature and Kiswahili reviewers. Establish publisher and archive agreements and a diverse selection across era, form and language. Require stable identity evidence, work/edition separation, a reviewed synopsis and genre, and a verified access link where possible. Track known/unknown language and original-language accuracy separately. Begin a set of at least 30 human-reviewed discovery queries. Hold ambiguous UUID mappings and unlicensed assets outside the release.
3. **Expansion to 500+ as a target.** Contract or document repeatable bulk access, automate version checks and feed diffs, add editorial sampling for each new language and provider, and stage reversible UUID reconciliation. Audit imbalance across authors, forms, publishers and languages, including titles absent from international sources. Set staffing and cost limits before scheduling expansion.
4. **Content experiment after rights clearance.** Test excerpt and full-text variants on an authorized subset. Retain synopsis and reviewed tags for titles outside it. Legal risk includes licences that cover reading without derivative processing, territorial limits, and translators or illustrations held by separate rights owners. Technical risks include source drift, incorrect groupings, missing languages and ranking bias toward richly described works. Keep those as explicit review gates.
