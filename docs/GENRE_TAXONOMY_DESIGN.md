# SomaRec literary vocabulary v1.0.0

The initial vocabulary has 29 original local concepts: 20 genres, seven forms and two audience groups. The scope includes fictional genres, life writing, criticism and prison poetry. The sample exercises only part of the vocabulary. Kiswahili labels are editorial drafts for review by a Kiswahili literature specialist. [Versioned vocabulary](../experiments/catalog_v2/editorial/taxonomy.v1.json)

## Architecture and compatibility

The frozen builder takes one coarse seed form per work (including `children`, `fiction` and `nonfiction`). Its edition language evidence comes from explicitly linked edition records. Phase 4 inherits that form, combines edition languages, applies literary proposals, then binds human decisions to the digest of the value, origin, evidence URLs, status and note. Its evaluator indexes eligible claims in the nine existing profile fields. Those behaviors determine where the extension attaches. [Catalog builder](../backend/somarec/catalog_v2.py) · [Editorial builder](../backend/somarec/editorial_review.py) · [Evaluation reader](../backend/somarec/catalog_evaluation.py)

Each work profile can now carry `taxonomy_version`, `genres` and `audiences` alongside `fields`. Each assignment is a normal editorial assertion. Its value contains the stable category ID and a display designation. The enclosing profile supplies the work ID. At most one genre can be primary. An empty list means no evidenced assignment yet. The ID identifies the concept independently of translations and display order. One work can have both historical-fiction and family-saga claims, each with its own evidence and reviewer decision. A genre assignment never populates the legacy `primary_genre` string. That field can reflect a distributor's shelving convention while the assignment reflects literary scholarship. A later editor must reconcile their display. The new schema reads Phase 4 artifacts. Consumers enforcing the *old* closed schema need the additive schema before opening new profiles. [Profile schema](../experiments/catalog_v2/editorial/profile.schema.json)

The existing proposal layer runs first. A second, version-controlled set of literary corrections supplies the old value, its exact assertion fingerprint, the new value, a reason and source URLs. It passes through the same replacement check. A changed rationale or source invalidates the resulting decision. A conflicting field cannot be replaced through this layer. Unaffected fields retain their fingerprints. For controlled assignments the existing decision key is `genres/<category_id>` or `audiences/<category_id>`. The work ID, field key and assertion fingerprint together identify the decision. The normal four statuses and conflict rules apply. Empty lists have no individual assertion to approve. The diagnostic evaluator still reads only the original fields. Changes to their draft content can change its observed results. [Workflow](CATALOG_V2_EDITORIAL.md)

## Vocabulary conventions

- The English preferred label is the canonical display name, and the ID is the durable key. Look up a preferred label or exact alias by dimension and language using Unicode NFC, case folding and whitespace normalization. English *poetry* and Kiswahili *ushairi* resolve to the same form. Within the prison-poetry genre, *ushairi wa gerezani* and *mashairi ya gerezani* resolve to the same ID. Broad *hadithi* is too ambiguous to register as an exact synonym of *short story*. A trope, subject or translation that only partly overlaps a concept needs a separate decision.
- Parent IDs point to broader concepts **within the same dimension**. Detective fiction has parents for crime and mystery. Parents enable browsing and do not imply additional approved assignments. Memoir is available as a life-writing genre while the Phase 4 form field can still contain the legacy word. Poetry as a form and prison poetry as a genre can coexist. Political repression describes subject matter. Children describes readership. Audience evidence does not establish a specific age band.
- Form, genre, theme, subject, setting, mood/tone, audience, language and original language are separately recognized dimensions. V1 populates just the first two and audience. Existing free-text literary assertions and source-specific edition language codes continue to carry the other dimensions.
- Add a category by editing the JSON with a new immutable ID, preferred labels, language-scoped aliases, dimension, parent IDs, provenance, status and a mappings list. Edit the vocabulary version for each published revision. Use patch revisions for label corrections, minor revisions for additive categories and major revisions for incompatible representation changes. For a changed meaning, create a new ID and deprecate the old one. Optional `replaced_by` links to an active same-dimension concept. Preserve historical snapshots in Git. New builds reject assignments to retired concepts until reviewed explicitly.
- External mapping relations are read **from the local concept toward the target**. The local poetry form has a close match to Wikidata Q482, whose definition also describes a literary style. Only reviewed Wikidata URI syntax is enabled in v1. Additional schemes need a rights check and identifier validation. A syntactically valid mapping still needs an editor to confirm equivalence. [Wikidata Q482](https://www.wikidata.org/wiki/Q482)

## Classification references and reuse

| Reference | Relevance to SomaRec | Reuse decision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| BISAC | Trade shelving categories often combine audience and subject. Useful for interpreting publisher records. | BISG distinguishes free online lookup from incorporating the list into systems under license. No headings list, hierarchy or code crosswalk was imported. [BISG FAQ](https://www.bisg.org/BISAC-FAQ) |
| Thema | Multilingual subject categories and independent qualifiers are relevant to audience and place. | EDItEUR licenses its copyrighted standard. Public documentation describes free general use subject to the license. Current first-party terms could not be retrieved in this session. Confirm the applicable version, adaptation and redistribution terms before any import or translation. [EDItEUR](https://www.editeur.org/151/Thema/) · [License DOI](https://doi.org/10.4400/nwqi) |
| Open Library | Its subject endpoint includes topics, persons, places and times. A provider heading can suggest a candidate for a specific dimension. | Open Library describes its catalog contributions under CC0 while acknowledging possible existing rights. A public catalog link supplies evidence. Covers, descriptions and underlying works require separate consideration. [Subjects API](https://openlibrary.org/dev/docs/api/subjects) · [Licensing](https://openlibrary.org/developers/licensing) |
| Wikidata | Stable entity IDs and multilingual labels can identify candidate concepts. Entity type matters for books and editions. | Structured data is CC0. Store a reviewed identifier and mapping relation. Linked creative works and non-structured text have separate terms. [Licensing](https://www.wikidata.org/wiki/Wikidata:Licensing) |
| Library of Congress | LCGFT describes genre/form; LCSH provides subject authorities. Their distinction helps separate what a work is from what it discusses. | Consult individual authority records and the relevant distribution terms before a crosswalk or redistribution. No authority files copied, no translations commissioned or presumed approved. [LCGFT](https://www.loc.gov/aba/publications/FreeLCGFT/freelcgft.html) · [LCSH](https://id.loc.gov/authorities/subjects.html) |

Independent review should check concept boundaries, Kiswahili terminology, ambiguous aliases and each proposed work assignment. The vocabulary is an offline editorial input. Expansion and release gates still require human decisions and relevance judgments. [Correction report](CATALOG_V2_CORRECTIONS.md)
